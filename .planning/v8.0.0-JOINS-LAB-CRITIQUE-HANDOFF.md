## 1. What the Joins Lab is (the proposed design)

A scholar keeps **one anchor fragment in view** (image + numbered transcription) and drives the
app's **existing** search tools to find the fragments that physically **join** it.
**Human-in-the-loop: the scholar is the ranker and confirmer. There is NO automated join-finder.**
Both apps (web + desktop).

**Component A — Join Workbench (primary)**
- **JWB-01** dedicated "Join Workbench" tab/page (both apps)
- **JWB-02** "Find joins" entry from desktop ResultDialog + Browse (web+desktop); open-by-shelfmark too
- **JWB-03** pinned anchor (image + numbered transcription) stays in view while searching
- **JWB-04** show **existing/known joins** for the anchor (PGP + FJMS + user + community)
- **JWB-05** **conservative** `[`/`]` tear-side assist — suggests side/direction *only when clear*; silent otherwise; always overridable
- **JWB-06** seed a search from the anchor (selected line / torn word + direction: rest-of-line, line-above, lines-below, lines-above, prev/next page) into the **existing** search module (variants/fuzzy/Responsa/regex); fully editable
- **JWB-07** collect results as **candidates in a list**
- **JWB-08** **side-by-side compare** (anchor vs candidate; image + transcription) to confirm by eye
- **JWB-09** act on a confirmed join: existing **joins button** + **export** (clipboard/file) + **add to list** (optional: open-in-Puzzle — both apps have it)

**Component B — Search-support algorithms (secondary, independent)**
- **JSA-01** parallels seeded from the anchor passage
- **JSA-02** corpus-driven **suggest-then-search** completion (first/last N words → corpus offers completions → search)
- **JSA-03** `[`/`]`-aware **torn-word completion** (`…את הש[` → candidates beginning `[מים ואת הארץ`)

Full text + Future items + Out-of-scope: `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md`.

## 4. The real material nature — hard questions to press

This is the heart of the critique. Each is a genuine risk to the design, grounded in how Genizah
fragments actually behave.

### A. Bracket / tear-edge detection (JWB-05, JSA-03) — the biggest data risk
- Bracket conventions are **not standardized**. `[ ]` often marks **editorial reconstruction or a
  mid-line lacuna**, not a physical *edge* tear. Does `]` at a line end *reliably* mean "torn at the
  (left, in RTL) edge," or will it fire on lacunae, expansions, and uncertain readings?
- **Coverage:** the MiDRASH auto-transcriptions (the killer feature, the bulk of the corpus) likely
  transcribe *visible glyphs* and may insert **no editorial brackets at all**. So bracket-driven
  detection may only work on human PGP transcriptions — a small slice. **→ Recommend a corpus probe:
  what fraction of transcriptions actually carry line-edge `[`/`]`, split by source (PGP vs MiDRASH)?**
- **RTL geometry:** confirm the side mapping is correct for right-to-left lines and matches *physical*
  edges, not reading order.

### B. The directionality + line model (JWB-06, JOINS-F1)
- A **vertical tear splits every line mid-word** → near-zero shared *complete* words across the tear
  (the report's central finding: 0 shared 4-word phrases on the benchmark). So "rest of the line" on
  raw text will mostly *fail* without the parallels/citation bridge. Is it honest to ship the
  within-line move as a primary path?
- **Absolute line position isn't preserved** across fragments (different rulings, fragments start
  mid-page). How robust is a relative "word A at line i, word B at line i+4" really, vs. an
  attractive demo? (This is exactly why JOINS-F1 is spike-gated.)
- **Layout reality:** multi-column pages, marginalia, varying line counts, prose vs. poetry stichs —
  does a single-column "line" model survive contact with the corpus?

### C. The "right text, wrong fragment" tension (JSA-01/02/03) — fundamental
- For **canonical texts** (Bible, common Talmud, fixed liturgy) there are **many copies**. Parallels /
  completion find the *text*, not the *physical partner* — a Genesis fragment yields hundreds of Bible
  copies. How does the workbench disambiguate the true join from another copy?
- **The cruel inversion:** join value is **highest for unique documentary material** (letters, legal
  deeds, accounts, lists) — but those have **no parallels and no citations to bridge**. The
  algorithmic helpers (B) work **worst exactly where joins matter most**, and **best where they
  produce the most noise** (canonical, multi-copy). Does the design confront this, or quietly assume
  literary/liturgical fragments?

### D. Data coverage reality
- Only **~217K of ~256K** records have any transcription; many fragments are **image-only** → the
  text-driven hunt can't seed from them at all. On *how many* real targets do **both** sides even
  have transcriptions?
- **VS ~50% coverage** — fine as supplementary, but never "no visual match ⇒ no join."
- **Known-joins completeness** (JWB-04): FJMS scholarly joins are curated but partial; PGP fragments
  are document-level. How trustworthy is "here's what's already joined" as a starting baseline?

### E. How scholars actually *confirm* a join (JWB-08)
- Real confirmation leans on **physical** signals: same **hand/script**, **ruling**, **ink**, damage/
  tear pattern continuity, **page dimensions**, parchment vs. paper, and **recto-verso continuity**.
  Text is one signal among many. Does side-by-side *image + transcription* suffice, or must the
  compare pane also surface **measurements + material metadata** (which the app already has from FJMS)?
- **Recto/verso:** a true join must work on **both** sides. The anchor shows one side — how does the
  workbench handle the verso constraint and verso images?

### F. Unit & scope of "join"
- What's the unit — **fragment / folio / bifolio / leaf**? Joins differ: **same-leaf** (physical
  tear) vs. **consecutive leaves** (same quire) vs. **same codex, dispersed** across libraries. These
  need *different* hunt strategies and *different* evidence. Which does v8.0.0 target first?
