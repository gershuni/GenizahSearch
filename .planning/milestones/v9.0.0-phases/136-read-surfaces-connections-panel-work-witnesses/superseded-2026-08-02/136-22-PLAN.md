<objective>
Settle the two decisions the evidence view cannot be built without: what text is rendered as the
reference side, and what the b-side of a page relation shows when it has no stored span.

Purpose: both were deliberately left open. The staged reference bodies are lossy by design — markup
stripped, rubrics and instructional lines dropped, vowel points and punctuation removed at
acquisition. That is arguably fine for comparison against unvocalised manuscript text, but it is not
the edition as published, and showing it without saying so would misrepresent someone else's work.
Separately, shared-text rows deliberately carry no b-side offsets, so when the viewed page is the
b-side there is nothing to highlight — a permanent structural fact, not a retryable error.
Output: an evidence pack and a dated decision record.
</objective>

  <decision>Two decisions: (1) what text does the reference side of the evidence view render — the stripped staged body, or display text re-fetched at render time? (2) what does the evidence view show when the viewed page is the b-side of a page relation and has no stored span?</decision>
  <context>
    (1) RENDER SOURCE. The staged bodies were stripped at acquisition — markup, rubrics and
    instructional lines, vowel points, cantillation and punctuation are gone. Against unvocalised
    manuscript text that is arguably the right comparison view, and it costs nothing at render time.
    But it is not the edition as published, so if it is shown, the surface should say what it is
    showing. Re-fetching display text keeps fidelity, adds a runtime dependency on a third party, and
    needs a defined behaviour when that dependency is down. Report 2 gives the concrete cost and
    failure behaviour; Report 1 gives the magnitude of what is lost.

    Note that this only affects works whose licence permits rendering at all, which Report 3 sizes.
    For every other work the reference side shows nothing regardless.

    (2) B-SIDE FORM. A page relation records the span on one side only, by design. When the reader is
    looking at the other page there is nothing to highlight. This is a permanent structural fact, not
    a transient failure, so it should not be worded as something a retry would fix. The options are to
    show the relation with its statistics and an explicit note that the passage location on this side
    was not recorded; to omit the evidence affordance entirely on b-side rows; or to show the a-side's
    span from the other manuscript, clearly labelled as belonging to the other page.

    Related and cheap to settle at the same time: when a row's stated matched-letter count exceeds
    what the single stored span can highlight, does the surface qualify the count, or state that it is
    showing one span of several? Report 3 sizes it.
  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="render-stripped">
      <name>Render the stripped staged text, and say so</name>
      <pros>No runtime dependency, no per-view cost, no failure mode; the comparison is against unvocalised manuscript text anyway</pros>
      <cons>It is not the edition as published; the surface carries an extra sentence explaining what it is showing</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="render-refetch">
      <name>Re-fetch display text at render time</name>
      <pros>Fidelity to the published edition; attribution sits beside the real text</pros>
      <cons>A third-party runtime dependency on an evidence view, with a per-view cost and a defined-but-real failure path</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="bside-note">
      <name>B-side shows the relation with a permanent structural note</name>
      <pros>The reader learns what exists and why nothing is highlighted; the row keeps its evidence affordance</pros>
      <cons>An affordance that opens to a note rather than a highlight may read as a failure unless the wording is careful</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="bside-omit">
      <name>B-side rows carry no evidence affordance</name>
      <pros>Nothing opens to nothing</pros>
      <cons>The reader cannot tell a b-side row from a row whose evidence is missing for another reason</cons>
